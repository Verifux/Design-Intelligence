# Taste Arbitrage

## The thesis

On 2026-07-16 a LinkedIn post made the case that AI has broken something in the tech job market. Instagram, Sesame, Serval, and Metric Geo were all paying $278K to $300K for plain Product Designer roles, not senior, principal, or staff, more than double the roughly $120K average. The reason: AI made shipping code and UI so easy that engineering stopped being the bottleneck. The new bottleneck is taste, meaning knowing what to build, for whom, and why, and making something that feels human in a market flooding with AI generated sameness. The inversion is stark. Software engineering postings are down 33% since 2020, CS grad unemployment sits at 6.1%, while design has plateaued but looks set to recover, per Lenny Rachitsky's research.

The comment thread sharpened the thesis past the headline. AI solves the blank canvas problem, meaning it speeds up exploring directions, but it does not replace the judgment needed to choose the right one. Beautiful UI is becoming a commodity. The real competitive edge is the experience: understanding users, context, workflows, and emotions well enough to solve the right problem. Several people warned that skipping real user research to jump straight onto the AI train produces polished but sloppy products with no data behind them. The strongest designers connect user needs, business goals, and technical constraints before anything gets polished. One line captured the mechanism cleanly: when outcome becomes cheaper, craft becomes valuable. Coding used to be the costliest part of the product lifecycle. Now it is not, so the value moved to whoever has the judgment to decide what is worth building.

Call this dynamic taste arbitrage. Execution is now a commodity anyone can buy cheaply with AI. Judgment did not get cheaper. The gap between those two prices is where the value now sits, and it is a gap worth naming explicitly in how a design practice describes itself.

## What this does not mean

This is not an anti AI stance and not a license to skip visual polish. Craft and taste still show up as good UI and good UX, they just are not sufficient on their own anymore. The differentiator is the decision trail behind the polish, not the absence of polish.

Keep this thesis's meaning of AI separate from any product specific meaning of AI experience. For example, Verifux's AIX pillar asks whether a product is legible to AI agents and LLM crawlers, which is a completely different concept from AI making UI generation cheap for humans. Never let a sentence be readable as both at once. If a piece of copy mentions AI, check which sense is meant and make sure only that sense is present.

## The weak versus strong copy test

Run any positioning line, case study paragraph, or proposal sentence through these five questions.

1. Does it lead with an aesthetic claim such as beautiful, modern, or clean, with no problem named behind it. That is a weak, commodity signal.
2. Does it name a specific user, workflow, or business consequence before it describes the interface. That is a strong, judgment signal.
3. Could a competitor running the same AI tools produce the exact same sentence. If yes, the sentence is describing the commodity layer, not the differentiator.
4. Does it show the decision trail, meaning research, then reframing, then building, or does it only show the shipped artefact. Trail is strong. Artefact only is weak.
5. Does it connect user need, business goal, and technical constraint in the same paragraph, rather than three separate ones. Connected is strong, separated is weak.

## How to apply

For positioning or homepage copy, lead with the diagnosis or problem framing claim and push how it looks to a supporting clause, never the headline.

For case studies, open with the judgment call that was made, meaning why this problem, why now, and what was rejected, then show the shipped result as proof, not as the pitch. Never invent a rejected alternative or an outcome that is not factually known. State it only where it is real and verifiable.

For proposals and pitch decks, name the specific ambiguity or misdiagnosis risk being solved before mentioning execution speed or which AI tooling was used.

Anywhere AI appears in copy, disambiguate which sense is meant: the tool used to build something, a product being legible to AI agents, or AI commoditizing a category. Never leave a sentence that could be read more than one way.

## Worked example

Weak: We design clean, modern interfaces for retailers.

Strong: We find the checkout step that is costing a retailer real revenue, then design the fix, not just the screen.

## When to invoke this skill

Use this whenever writing or reviewing Designerama or Verifux marketing copy, drafting or reframing portfolio case studies, drafting client proposals or pitch decks, or auditing existing copy for the looks good but says nothing risk.
